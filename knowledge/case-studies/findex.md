## **Summary of the business**

Findex operates at scale, with diverse service offerings, large sales and advisory teams, and a strong focus on governance and compliance. Their CRM needed to support this complexity while remaining intuitive for daily use. The goal was not just a platform migration, but a fundamental reset of how sales and marketing teams operate and collaborate.

## **The problem**

### **A CRM that reflected complexity but hindered adoption**

Professional services organisations like Findex often outgrow traditional CRM setups. Over time, systems become overly customised, difficult to maintain, and misaligned with how teams actually work. Findex’s previous Salesforce environment delivered technical capability, but at the cost of usability and visibility.

### **Fragmented visibility across services and business units**

Findex’s service catalogue spans multiple service lines, families, and offerings, often bundled together within a single client engagement. Representing this accurately in a CRM, while supporting forecasting, reporting, and automation, required more than standard deal pipelines.

## **The solution**

Engaging.io delivered a structured HubSpot onboarding tailored for a complex, service-led enterprise. HubSpot was selected not as a lightweight CRM, but as a scalable platform capable of supporting advanced data models, automation, and enterprise reporting when architected correctly.

Following in-depth discovery across sales, marketing, and technical stakeholders, we designed a HubSpot architecture that balanced native capability with extensibility through Operations Hub and custom configuration.

Sales Hub Enterprise was configured with dual pipelines for advisory and audit teams, including custom properties, automation, lead routing, and reporting dashboards. Marketing Hub Enterprise and Content Hub Professional enabled modular assets and a unified subscription model across multiple brands.

To support Findex’s layered service structure, we built a custom Service Catalogue using HubSpot UI Extensions. This enabled services to be organised by service line and family, maintaining structured SKU relationships aligned to their internal hierarchy. It provided the flexibility required without compromising usability.

We then implemented Ticket-based automation to operationalise service delivery and revenue tracking. Each selected service triggers the automatic creation of tickets, tasks, and activities, capturing key forecast and actual revenue dates and amounts. This standardised execution and enabled accurate reporting across pipeline, delivery, and financial performance.

Operations Hub Professional underpinned data governance and automation, positioning HubSpot as the operational front end while supporting secure data exchange with internal systems. Complex logic sits behind the scenes, allowing teams to focus on clients while leadership gains structured visibility into revenue and performance.

## **The strategic plan**

**Deliver early value within a strict 120-day window**
With a defined 120-day out clause, the initial phase focused on visible, practical impact rather than edge-case perfection. We prioritised core sales and marketing workflows, dual pipelines, structured reporting, and foundational automation that teams would use daily. This ensured HubSpot delivered credible value quickly, built internal confidence, and created momentum for broader transformation.

**Design HubSpot around services, not just deals**
Findex operates as a service-led organisation, with engagements spanning multiple service lines, families, and advisory disciplines. Instead of compressing this complexity into a deal-only model, we designed a scalable service hierarchy aligned to their real commercial structure. Through a custom UI Extension, services and SKUs were organised in a structured catalogue that reflected how work is actually sold and delivered. This ensured forecasting, reporting, and automation were based on true service activity rather than simplified proxies.

**Operationalise delivery and revenue through ticket automation**
To bridge the gap between sales and delivery, we used the Ticket object to automate service execution. Each selected service triggers predefined tickets, tasks, and activities, capturing forecast signed, billed, completed, and paid dates alongside revenue amounts. This standardised execution across business units while enabling accurate reporting on forecast versus actual performance.

**Support multiple business units with controlled governance**
Given Findex’s multi-unit structure, the plan balanced autonomy with consistency. Clear ownership models, permission frameworks, and reporting conventions were implemented to allow teams to operate independently without fragmenting data. Leadership retained cross-business visibility while reducing internal friction.

**Align CRM data with the internal data lake**
HubSpot was positioned as the operational front end, not a replacement for Findex’s data lake. We prioritised data quality, consistent identifiers, and integration readiness to ensure secure, scalable data exchange. This approach protects long-term reporting maturity and avoids future architectural rework.

**Enable adoption through structured training and documentation**
Adoption was treated as a design priority. Role-based training, documented workflows, and structured enablement ensured users understood both how to use the system and why processes were configured as they were. This embedded HubSpot into daily operations rather than layering it on top of existing behaviours.

## **The implementation**

### **Discovery and solution design**

We began with structured discovery workshops across sales, marketing, operations, and technical stakeholders to document end-to-end processes, service hierarchies, data dependencies, and reporting requirements. The focus was on understanding how advisory and audit work is actually sold and delivered, rather than replicating legacy system configurations. This resulted in detailed process maps, defined data models, and solution design documentation that acted as a single source of truth throughout delivery.

In complex professional services environments, this alignment phase is non-negotiable. By agreeing upfront on workflow stages, ownership models, service structures, and reporting outputs, we minimised rework, reduced scope drift, and ensured HubSpot was configured to support real-world operations from day one.

**Custom service catalogue via UI extensions**
To reflect Findex’s multi-layered service model, we developed a structured Service Catalogue using HubSpot UI Extensions. This provided a hierarchical service selection interface aligned to internal service lines and families, embedding service IDs and structured relationships directly into HubSpot. Rather than forcing complexity into standard product fields, the custom interface ensured services were selected consistently, reported accurately, and aligned with Findex’s commercial taxonomy.

**Ticket-driven revenue and delivery automation**
We then operationalised service delivery using the Ticket object. When a service is selected, predefined tickets, tasks, and activities are automatically created, standardising execution across advisory and audit teams. These tickets capture forecast signed, completed, billed, and paid dates, alongside associated revenue amounts. Reporting dashboards were structured to compare forecast versus actual revenue performance, giving leadership visibility into both pipeline timing and delivery milestones.

**Future-proofing and scalability**
The architecture was intentionally designed for growth. New services and SKUs can be added into the hierarchy without redevelopment, with reporting fields standardised across service types to maintain consistency. Workflows were built to support additional automation layers as Findex expands, ensuring the platform can evolve without structural rework.

This implementation approach ensured HubSpot was not only configured correctly, but engineered as a scalable revenue operations foundation aligned to how Findex operates in practice.

### **Training and enablement**

User acceptance was treated as a core success metric, not an afterthought. A structured train-the-trainer model was implemented to build internal capability and ownership across teams. Key users were trained deeply on both how HubSpot works and why specific processes were designed the way they were.

Training sessions were supported with recorded walkthroughs, written documentation, and tailored HubSpot Academy pathways. This approach ensured new users could ramp up quickly, reduced reliance on external support, and helped embed HubSpot into day-to-day operations rather than positioning it as a standalone system.

Importantly, training was timed to align with real usage. Teams were onboarded close to go-live, with scenarios and examples drawn directly from their roles, increasing confidence and early adoption.

## **Impact and results**

The introduction of a custom Service Catalogue and ticket-driven automation transformed how Findex manages services, revenue visibility, and cross-functional execution. HubSpot is no longer simply a CRM layer. It now reflects the scale and sophistication of Findex’s service-led operating model.

In the short term, teams gained immediate structural clarity. Services are organised logically within a defined hierarchy, selected consistently at the deal level, and automatically supported by ticket-driven task generation. Manual administration has reduced materially, and service delivery is standardised across advisory and audit business units. Advisors can clearly see which services are in scope for each opportunity, track complex engagements with confidence, and rely on automation to manage handoffs and status updates. This has improved pipeline consistency and reduced variability in how opportunities are progressed and reported.

From a revenue operations perspective, the shift has been significant. By linking structured service configuration to automated ticket creation and controlled revenue fields, Findex can now report with precision on forecast signed dates versus actual completion, forecast billed and paid dates, estimated versus realised revenue, and delivery milestones aligned directly to commercial outcomes. This closes a common gap between pipeline forecasting and operational revenue recognition. Leadership now has real-time visibility into when revenue is expected, when it is delivered, and how performance tracks against forecast across multiple business units.

Marketing capability has also matured. Teams manage campaigns, segmentation, and content directly within HubSpot using modular templates and a unified subscription model. Multi-brand communications are executed from a single governed platform, reducing reliance on external agencies for day-to-day execution while accelerating turnaround on campaigns and reporting. This has strengthened alignment between sales activity, service delivery, and marketing performance.

At the leadership level, HubSpot provides consistent, trusted reporting across pipelines, services, and campaigns. Clear permission structures maintain governance without restricting autonomy, and the underlying data architecture supports ongoing integration with Findex’s internal data lake. The platform is positioned as a scalable operational front end within a broader enterprise data ecosystem.

Long term, the architecture ensures growth does not introduce friction. New service lines and SKUs can be added to the structured hierarchy without destabilising reporting. Automation scales alongside expansion without increasing administrative burden.

By selecting HubSpot and partnering with Engaging.io, Findex demonstrated that enterprise complexity does not require enterprise friction. With the right strategy, architecture, and delivery discipline, HubSpot has become a best-fit solution for a modern financial services organisation, delivering measurable operational gains today while providing a durable foundation for long-term scale.
